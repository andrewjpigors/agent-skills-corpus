# Seller Conversation Script — BC Market

> **Jurisdiction:** Licensed **BC REALTORS®** operating in **British Columbia** only.
> All statutory references are BC-specific. Nothing in this script is legal or tax advice.

Produce a structured, evidence-based conversation script for a listing review or
price-reduction discussion with a seller client — **after** all mandatory regulatory
gates have been cleared and documented.

**This skill is standalone.** It does not require Skill 09 (Should You Sell Now). All
tax and ownership flags are embedded here as mandatory gates.

---

## ⚠ SECURITY AND PRIVACY WARNINGS

**Read before generating any script.**

### Script Security
Scripts generated by this skill **must not** contain:
- The seller's actual minimum acceptable price, walk-away number, or private price floor
- Any language revealing the seller's motivation, financial urgency, or timeline pressure
- References to the seller's mortgage balance, financial stress, or personal circumstances

**Why this matters:** Seller conversation scripts are sometimes shared digitally,
saved to cloud drives, or accidentally visible to buyer-side agents. Any negotiating
position embedded in the script is an information leak that can directly harm the
seller's position and expose the licensee to a breach-of-confidence claim under RESA
Rules s.30(e) (duty to maintain confidentiality).

When the seller's actual price floor is relevant to framing the conversation, use
placeholder language: *"your private price floor"* or *"[CONFIDENTIAL — not for
script]"* — never the number itself.

### Privacy
The seller's financial situation is sensitive personal information. This skill does
not record, store, or include seller financial details in the script output beyond
what is market-facing and publicly defensible.

---

## What This Skill Does

- Runs mandatory regulatory screening **before** any pricing language is produced
- Generates a structured price conversation script calibrated to the seller's situation
- Flags tax, ownership, DRPO, and legal matters that must be resolved before the
  price discussion proceeds
- Ensures all pricing language is evidence-based and seller-authorized

What this skill **never** does:
- Issue any tax conclusion, even an "obvious" one
- Tell a seller they "won't owe tax," "qualify for an exemption," or "are fine"
- Produce pricing language before the tax screening gate is cleared
- Generate a script that commits the seller to a price without written authorization
- Include the seller's negotiating position or financial motivation in the script body

### GATE 1 — Commission-Interest Conflict Screen (RESA Rules s.30(a), (i), (j))

**The commission-interest conflict risk.** A licensee who has a financial stake in a
transaction closing is not a neutral advisor. RESA Rules s.30(a) requires the licensee
to act in the **best interests of the client** — not in the licensee's own commission
interest. Where the licensee's recommendation to reduce price (or hold price) could be
influenced by their need to earn a commission, s.30(a) is engaged.

**BCFSA enforcement precedent — Hu (2024):** BCFSA cancelled the licence of Alan
LanFang Hu and his personal real estate corporation and ordered them to pay a joint
discipline penalty of **$120,000**. Hu used confidential information obtained from his
buyer client to facilitate his own purchase of the same property, collected a referral
fee without disclosure, and provided false statements to the regulator. BC Supreme Court
(Francis J.) ordered Hu to disgorge $626,000+ in profits and repay the commission.
BCFSA found violations of RESA Rules s.30(a), (e), (f), (i), (j) and s.33.
*Conduct unbecoming a licensee under RESA s.35(1)(a), (e), (g) and s.35(2)(a)–(c).*

**The lesson for seller conversations:** If the licensee stands to benefit financially
from a price change (e.g., closing a stale listing for their commission), the pricing
recommendation must be grounded in documented market evidence — not in the licensee's
financial interest. The Hu precedent demonstrates that BCFSA treats self-interest over
client interest as licence-ending conduct.

**Before generating any script, answer:**

| Question | If YES — Required Action |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Do you currently represent, or have you recently represented, a buyer who considered this property? | ⛔ STOP — Written conflict-of-interest disclosure to seller AND managing broker required before this conversation. Use the conflict disclosure form. See RESA Rules s.30(j) (as amended Feb 1, 2024, effective July 1, 2024). |
| Does the licensee have a personal financial interest in this property or a related transaction? | ⛔ STOP — Written conflict-of-interest disclosure and managing broker consultation required immediately. |
| Is the licensee receiving any referral fee, co-operative commission, or other remuneration from any party other than through their brokerage? | ⛔ STOP — Dual remuneration is prohibited without client disclosure (Rules s.30(f)). |

**Dual agency abolition reminder (Rules ss. 63–65, Division 4, Part 5):**
Dual agency is prohibited in BC. The only exception is a narrow remote/underserved-
location exemption (s.64) that does not apply in Greater Vancouver or any urban market.
If representing both seller and any buyer on this property would constitute dual agency,
the licensee must choose one client or release both (s.65). The "Disclosure of Risks
Associated with Dual Agency" prescribed form must be provided if the narrow exemption
is invoked. Designated agency (s.32) is the normal structure — it does not constitute
dual agency provided each designated agent represents only one party.

**Confidential information from prior buyer relationships:** Even if no current buyer
conflict exists, the licensee must confirm out loud that confidential information from
any prior buyer client relationship involving this property will not inform this
conversation, any pricing recommendation, or any listing strategy. This obligation
survives the termination of buyer representation (Rules s.30(e)).

### GATE 2 — DRPO Offer-Presentation Obligations (effective March 17, 2025)

**IDRPO is retired.** The Irrevocable Direction Regarding Presentation of Offers
(IDRPO) is no longer in use as of March 17, 2025. It has been replaced by the
**Direction Regarding Presentation of Offers (DRPO)**, which is revocable.

**Why this matters in seller conversations:** The DRPO governs how and when offers
are presented to the seller. Seller conversations often involve pricing strategy
discussions that intersect with offer-presentation decisions. Any price strategy
discussion must be framed within the seller's current DRPO instructions.

**DRPO obligations (REBGV / FVREB / CADREB Rules of Cooperation, March 2025):**

| Obligation | Requirement |
|------------|-------------|
| Form required | A completed DRPO signed by the seller must be on file for every listing |
| MLS upload | Listing brokerage must upload signed DRPO within 24 hours of listing activation or form completion |
| Default rule | Offers must be presented to the seller **without delay** unless DRPO specifies otherwise |
| Delayed presentation | If seller selects a future date/time, exact wording *"No presentation of offers until [date/time]"* must appear in **both** Public and REALTOR® Remarks |
| Pre-emptive (bully) offer | If seller wants to consider a pre-emptive offer before the scheduled date: (1) obtain **revised DRPO** signed by seller; (2) update MLS listing; (3) notify ALL cooperating brokerages that showed, requested showings, requested offer presentation, or requested updates — and give them **equal and concurrent** opportunity to present |
| Cooperating attendance | Unless seller explicitly restricts it (noted in REALTOR® Remarks), cooperating brokerages have the right to attend offer presentation |
| Record retention | All offers and communications must be retained for minimum 1 year |

**BCFSA rationale:** BCFSA confirmed that licensees bound by an irrevocable direction
would face an irreconcilable conflict of interest if their client wanted to reverse the
direction. RESA requires licensees to act in accordance with the lawful instructions of
their client (Rules s.30(b)). The DRPO resolves this by making seller instructions
revocable at any time.

**In the seller conversation:** If the seller asks about changing their offer
presentation strategy (e.g., moving up an offer date, accepting a bully offer):
> "Any change to your offer presentation instructions requires a revised DRPO form
> signed by you. I'll also need to update the MLS listing immediately and notify all
> cooperating brokerages who have shown interest — they need equal opportunity to
> present. I can walk you through exactly what that process looks like."

### COMMERCIAL FLAG — Commercial Property ⚠ IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

> ⚠ COMMERCIAL PROPERTY — DIFFERENT RULES APPLY: The following distinctions apply
> to commercial real estate in BC and Canada.
>
> **GST:** The sale of commercial real property is generally a **taxable supply** under
> the Excise Tax Act — GST applies (5%). Unlike most residential resales, there is no
> automatic exemption. If the seller is a GST registrant, they must collect and remit
> GST — unless the purchaser is also registered and accounts for it directly (joint
> election). Failure to handle GST correctly exposes the seller to CRA liability.
>
> **Principal Residence Exemption:** Commercial sellers do not qualify for the PRE.
> All gain is a capital gain (or business income) without the PRE shield.
>
> **BC Home Flipping Tax:** Commercial property is exempt from BC Home Flipping Tax
> **only** if it was used **exclusively** for a commercial purpose for the **entire**
> hold period. A home office inside a residential unit does not qualify. Mixed use
> (e.g., ground-floor commercial / upper-floor residential) must be assessed carefully.
>
> **Capital Gains:** Capital gains inclusion rate is currently 50% (proposed increase
> to 66.7% was cancelled March 21, 2025 by PM Carney). This remains the operative rate.
>
> **Referral required:** This client must consult a tax accountant and solicitor
> regarding GST, capital gains, and any applicable depreciation recapture before
> listing. This is outside licensee expertise (RESA Rules s.30(d)).

### GATE 4 — Pre-Script Tax Screening (RESA Rules s.30(d))

**Runs before any pricing language is generated — no exceptions.**

Answer all six questions. A "yes" to any question triggers the corresponding flag.

| # | Screening Question | If YES — Required Action |
|---|-------------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Has the seller owned this property for fewer than 365 days? | ⛔ STOP — FLAG 1 (Federal + BC flipping tax stack). Tax referral required before any price or timing decision. |
| 2 | Has the seller owned this property for 365–729 days? | ⚠ FLAG 2 (BC Home Flipping Tax graduated rate still live). Tax referral required. |
| 3 | Is the seller a non-resident of Canada for tax purposes? | ⛔ STOP — FLAG 3 (ITA s.116 withholding). Solicitor + tax accountant required immediately. |
| 4 | Is this an estate sale or does the seller hold through a corporation, bare trust, or partnership? | ⚠ FLAG 4 (Estate) or FLAG 5 (LOTA/entity structure). Legal + tax referral required before listing. |
| 5 | Has the property had any rental or business use, or has CCA (depreciation) ever been claimed? | ⚠ FLAG 6 (Capital gains / CCA recapture). Tax referral required. |
| 6 | Is this property in a Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) designated area? | ⚠ FLAG 7 (SVT lien check). Confirm SVT declaration is current before listing or accepting offers. |

---

### FLAG 1 — Property Held ≤ 365 Days ⛔ CRITICAL

> ⛔ CRITICAL — TAX REFERRAL REQUIRED BEFORE ANY PRICING OR TIMING DISCUSSION:
>
> **Federal rule (ITA s.12(12), in force January 1, 2023):** A residential property
> held for fewer than 365 consecutive days is a "flipped property." The entire gain is
> deemed to be business income — fully taxable at marginal rates. The principal
> residence exemption does not apply. There are limited exemptions for life events
> (death, serious illness, disability, involuntary job loss, divorce/separation, etc.),
> but the burden is on the taxpayer to establish eligibility.
>
> **BC Home Flipping Tax (Residential Property (Short-Term Holding) Profit Tax Act,
> effective January 1, 2025):** A 20% tax applies on net taxable income from the sale.
> The primary residence deduction is capped at $20,000 (not a full exemption) and is
> only available if the seller owned and lived in the property for at least 365
> consecutive days — which by definition has not occurred here. A BC Home Flipping Tax
> return must be filed within **90 days of the sale date**, regardless of whether any
> tax is owing. Non-filing attracts penalties.
>
> **These taxes stack and cannot offset each other:** The BC Home Flipping Tax cannot
> be deducted against federal income (neither as business income nor capital gains
> deduction). Both apply simultaneously.
>
> **Capital gains inclusion rate:** The proposed increase to 66.7% was permanently
> cancelled on March 21, 2025. The current rate is 50% — but this is irrelevant here
> because a flipped property is taxed as business income at full marginal rates, not
> as a capital gain.
>
> This client must consult a tax accountant or tax lawyer **before any list price or
> timing decision is made.** Do not include any pricing language in this script until
> that advice has been obtained. This is outside licensee expertise (RESA Rules s.30(d)).

---

### FLAG 2 — Property Held 365–729 Days ⚠ HIGH PRIORITY

> ⚠ TAX REFERRAL REQUIRED:
>
> **BC Home Flipping Tax:** The BC Home Flipping Tax remains live on a graduated scale.
> The rate is calculated as: **20% × [1 - (Days held - 365) / 365]**. It does not
> reach zero until day 730. At 366 days the rate is approximately 19.95%; at 500 days
> it is approximately 9.04%. The primary residence deduction of up to $20,000 may be
> available if the seller lived in the property for the entire hold period.
>
> **Filing requirement:** A BC Home Flipping Tax return must be filed within **90 days
> of the sale date**, even if an exemption is being claimed and no tax is owed.
> Non-filing attracts penalties.
>
> **Federal rule (ITA s.12(12)):** The federal anti-flipping rule does NOT apply at
> this hold period (it expires at 365 days). However, CRA may still characterize the
> gain as business income under common law factors — particularly if there is evidence
> of a commercial intent. This requires professional assessment.
>
> This client must obtain tax advice before proceeding. The licensee cannot determine
> the applicable rate, available deductions, or filing obligations. This is outside
> licensee expertise (RESA Rules s.30(d)).

---

### FLAG 3 — Non-Resident Seller ⛔ CRITICAL

> ⛔ CRITICAL — NON-RESIDENT REFERRAL REQUIRED BEFORE LISTING:
>
> **ITA s.116 — Withholding obligation:** If the seller is a non-resident of Canada for
> tax purposes, the buyer is legally required to withhold a portion of gross proceeds
> at closing. Without a Clearance Certificate:
> - Standard residential property: buyer withholds **25%** of gross proceeds
> - Depreciable property (e.g., rental buildings): buyer withholds **50%**
>
> The withheld amount is remitted to CRA within 30 days after the end of the month of
> closing. If the buyer fails to withhold and remit, the buyer becomes personally liable
> to CRA for the amount that should have been withheld — this can make the listing
> unmarketable or create a significant buyer deterrence.
>
> **Clearance Certificate timeline:** CRA currently processes Clearance Certificate
> applications in **4–6 months** on average (some files take longer depending on
> complexity). The application should be initiated well before the anticipated closing
> date. The seller's Canadian solicitor or tax accountant must initiate this immediately.
>
> **Comfort letter option:** If the Clearance Certificate will not arrive before closing,
> the seller's solicitor and buyer's solicitor can arrange a holdback in trust pending
> receipt — but this requires advance coordination and both parties' agreement.
>
> **UHTA compliance link:** CRA may refuse to issue a Clearance Certificate if the
> non-resident seller has outstanding filing or payment obligations under the Underused
> Housing Tax Act (UHTA). UHTA compliance must be confirmed before applying for the
> Clearance Certificate.
>
> **Notification obligation:** The non-resident must notify CRA of the disposition
> within 10 days after the sale (penalty: $25/day, minimum $100, maximum $2,500 per
> property).
>
> This client must engage a **Canadian tax lawyer or accountant immediately** — before
> listing, and certainly before accepting any offer. This is outside licensee expertise
> (RESA Rules s.30(d)).

---

### FLAG 4 — Estate or Probate Sale ⚠ HIGH PRIORITY

> ⚠ TAX AND LEGAL REFERRAL REQUIRED:
>
> **ITA s.70(5) — Deemed disposition at death:** The deceased is deemed to have
> disposed of all capital property at fair market value immediately before death. Capital
> gains accrued to the date of death are reportable on the deceased's terminal T1 return
> or on the estate's T3 Trust Return, depending on timing and the nature of the property.
>
> **Executor personal liability:** An executor who distributes the estate before all
> taxes are paid may become personally liable for unpaid CRA obligations. Executors must
> obtain a Clearance Certificate from CRA before final distribution.
>
> **Probate requirement:** If the property has not yet been transmitted to the executor's
> name at LTO (Transmission to Personal Representative), the solicitor must complete
> that step before listing. An executor cannot sign listing contracts or a contract of
> purchase and sale on behalf of the estate until the title is properly transmitted.
>
> **LOTA:** A Transparency Declaration must be filed at LTO on the subsequent transfer.
> If the estate itself is a reporting body, a Transparency Report is also required.
>
> This client must consult both a **tax accountant and a solicitor** before listing.
> This is outside licensee expertise (RESA Rules s.30(d)).

---

### FLAG 5 — Corporate, Bare Trust, or Partnership Ownership ⚠ HIGH PRIORITY

> ⚠ LEGAL REFERRAL REQUIRED:
>
> **LOTA — Transparency Declaration:** Under the Land Owner Transparency Act, a
> Transparency Declaration must be filed with the LTO administrator at the time of any
> transfer of an interest in land. If the seller is a reporting body (corporation,
> trustee of a relevant trust, or partner of a relevant partnership), a Transparency
> Report disclosing all interest holders must also be filed. The LTO **will refuse** to
> register the transfer if the Transparency Declaration is missing or incomplete.
>
> **Penalties:** Failure to file a Transparency Report: up to $50,000 (corporation) or
> $25,000 (individual), or 15% of assessed property value, whichever is greater.
>
> **Retroactive compliance:** If the entity structure has not been previously declared
> under LOTA (e.g., a bare trust arrangement that was overlooked), retroactive compliance
> may be required before the transfer. This is a solicitor matter.
>
> **Tax implications:** Corporate sellers face different capital gains treatment; bare
> trust sellers may have different ACB calculations; partnership dispositions have
> specific allocation rules. All require professional tax advice.
>
> This client must engage a **solicitor** before the listing launches to confirm LOTA
> compliance and registration readiness. This is outside licensee expertise (RESA Rules
> s.30(d)).

---

### FLAG 6 — Rental or Business Use / CCA Claimed ⚠ HIGH PRIORITY

> ⚠ TAX REFERRAL REQUIRED:
>
> **Principal Residence Exemption limitation:** Any year the property was used as a
> rental or for business (where the change-in-use rules apply) may reduce the number of
> years eligible for the PRE. If the seller claimed Capital Cost Allowance (CCA/
> depreciation), that portion of the property is disqualified from the PRE in any event.
>
> **CCA recapture:** If CCA was claimed on the property, the recaptured amount is fully
> taxable as ordinary income — not as a capital gain — regardless of the PRE. This can
> create a significant and unexpected tax liability.
>
> **Change-in-use rules (ITA s.45):** A deemed disposition may have occurred when the
> property converted between personal and income-producing use. That deemed disposition
> date and value affects the ACB calculation and tax owing on the actual sale.
>
> This client must obtain advice from a **tax accountant** before any pricing or timing
> decision. This is outside licensee expertise (RESA Rules s.30(d)).

---

### FLAG 7 — Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) Lien Risk ⚠ HIGH PRIORITY

> ⚠ SVT COMPLIANCE REQUIRED BEFORE LISTING:
>
> **2026 SVT rates (effective for 2026 calendar year, declaration due March 31, 2026):**
> - Foreign owners and untaxed worldwide earners: **3%** of assessed value per year
>   (increased from 2% for 2019–2025)
> - Canadian citizens and permanent residents: **1%** of assessed value per year
>   (increased from 0.5% for 2019–2025)
>
> **Lien risk:** If SVT is unpaid, the Province may register a **lien on title** through
> the LTSA. An SVT lien appears on a title search and will block a property transfer.
> A buyer's solicitor conducting the standard title search will find it. The seller
> cannot close without clearing the lien — it must be paid out from sale proceeds.
>
> **Action before listing:** Confirm with the seller that their SVT annual declaration
> is current and that no outstanding balance exists. The seller's solicitor or
> accountant should confirm no lien appears on a current title search.
>
> **Property types subject to SVT:** Applies in designated taxable areas (Metro
> Vancouver, Greater Victoria, Kelowna, Abbotsford-Mission, Chilliwack, Nanaimo).
> Verify the property falls within a designated area before applying this flag.
>
> This is outside licensee expertise — refer to a **tax accountant or solicitor** for
> SVT compliance confirmation (RESA Rules s.30(d)).

### GATE 5 — Evidence Gate (RESA Rules s.30(a))

**Runs before any pricing language is generated.**

A pricing conversation requires documented, verifiable market evidence:
- CMA with source-cited comparable sales (address, sale date, price, sq ft, days on market)
- MLS DOM data for active and sold comparables in the relevant micro-market and time window
- List-to-sale ratios for the relevant sub-market (not Metro-wide averages)

Any pricing recommendation — including a recommendation to hold price — must be based
on documented market evidence (BCFSA Real Estate Services Rules s.30(a)). A script that
recommends a price change without this evidence is non-compliant. Fabricated or
estimated comparables are prohibited.

**Commission-interest evidence discipline:** Because the licensee has a financial
interest in closing, the evidence standard must be applied rigorously. The pricing
recommendation in the script must follow the evidence — not the licensee's commission
interest. If the evidence supports holding price, the script presents that case, even
though it is not in the licensee's short-term financial interest.

---

### GATE 6 — Strata Context

If this is a strata property: confirm the seller is current on strata fees. An
outstanding balance creates a Form F (Certificate of Payment) obligation at closing —
the strata corporation will not issue Form F without full payment, and the sale cannot
complete without it. The strata corporation may also sue the seller for the shortfall
independently. Flag this before listing.

## HARD RULES

1. **NEVER** include pricing language until all six gates are cleared and all triggered flags are output
2. **NEVER** advise the seller they "won't owe tax," "qualify for an exemption," or "are fine" on any tax matter
3. **NEVER** present a price reduction or increase as a unilateral agent decision — always frame as seller-authorized
4. **NEVER** include the seller's private minimum price, financial urgency, or negotiating position in the script body
5. **NEVER** reference verbal commitments or representations made outside the written listing contract
6. **NEVER** generate a script for a property where a conflict of interest has not been declared in writing first
7. **NEVER** omit any triggered regulatory flag from the script output
8. **ALWAYS** include the BCREA Price Change Authorization reminder when any price change is being discussed
9. **ALWAYS** include the verbatim referral language when any flag is triggered
10. **ALWAYS** present the evidence first, then the options — never lead with the conclusion
11. **ALWAYS** ensure the DRPO obligations are referenced if the seller raises offer-presentation strategy questions

## Step 3 — Describe the Seller Situation

Once all six gates are cleared and all triggered flags are printed, provide:

- Days on market
- Number of showings and feedback themes (without identifying individual buyers)
- Current list price
- Comparable sales data (per the Evidence Gate)
- Active competing listings: prices, DOM, key differences from subject property
- Seller's stated goal — timeline, price sensitivity (do not include private price floor in script body)
- Strata context if applicable (fees current, Form F status)
- Any known material facts about the property that affect pricing or disclosure
- Whether a DRPO is in place and what offer-presentation strategy it reflects

## Script Output Format

### Opening — Acknowledge Without Defending

Recognize the seller's experience (waiting, showing fatigue, uncertainty) before
presenting any data. No defensiveness about previous pricing advice.

### Evidence Presentation — Data, Not Opinion

Use the specific numbers provided. Not "the market is soft" — instead: "Three
comparable homes sold in [neighbourhood] in the last 90 days at $X, $X, and $X.
Yours is currently listed at $X, which is $Y above that range."

All data cited must match the documented evidence. No fabricated comparables or
estimated ranges. If a data point is an estimate, identify it as such.

DOM transparency: MLS displays cumulative DOM to all buyers and their agents. Extended
DOM is interpreted by the buyer pool as a pricing signal, not a uniqueness signal. The
script can reference this as a market reality if supported by evidence, not as pressure.

### Two Paths With Honest Trade-Offs

Present exactly two options with specific numbers and realistic, evidence-based outcomes:

- **Path A — Hold price:** realistic DOM expectation, buyer pool impact, competing
  inventory comparison
- **Path B — Adjust price:** the specific number, evidence basis, expected impact,
  realistic timeline

Neither path is framed as the "right" choice. The seller decides. The licensee's job
is to present the evidence, not advocate for the outcome that serves their commission.

### Price Change — Authorization Reminder

If Path B is selected, include verbatim:
> "Before we make any change to the list price, I'll send you a written authorization
> to sign using the BCREA Price Change Authorization form. That protects you and
> documents your decision. Nothing changes on MLS until I have that in writing."

### DRPO Review (if relevant)

If a delayed offer presentation date is in place, include:
> "Your current DRPO specifies [date/time] for offer presentation. If you'd like to
> change that for any reason — including to consider an offer that comes in before
> that date — we'd need to update your written instructions, update the MLS listing,
> and I'd need to notify all brokerages who have shown interest. Sellers retain full
> flexibility to revise these instructions at any time."

### Close — Specific Next Step and Timeline

A concrete action with a date. No open-ended "let's think about it."

### [PAUSE] Markers

Script includes `[PAUSE]` prompts at key moments where the seller should be heard
before the licensee continues. Never talk over a pause.

---

## Style

Calm, direct, evidence-based. No pressure. The data is presented; the seller decides.

BC market context: Metro Vancouver. Buyers are price-sensitive and have full access to
DOM history. Beyond 21 days on market, perceived value diminishes measurably in most
sub-markets — reference this as market-documented reality if DOM data supports it,
not as a pressure tactic.

Tone does not shift based on the licensee's commission interest. A script that advocates
for a price reduction the evidence does not support is a compliance risk.

This script is a professional consultation tool — not legal or tax advice.
